Summary

Japanese writes no spaces, and the browser's word splitter cuts inflected words short (食べました → 食|べ|ま|した). Clicking 食 showed "food". The server now finds the word around the clicked piece with a morphological analyzer (Lindera + UniDic). It underlines the word and lists its parts, each looked up by its base form: 食べ|まし|た → 食べる, ます, た. This needed a protocol change (segments may split the underlined word) and an extension fix, and it costs about 600 MB of RAM while ja_en is served.

Two gates pass. The content-word gate misses by 0.4 points (4 items), inside the judge's noise. Its remaining failures are mostly senses English Wiktionary lacks.

Accepted changes

Server (general mechanisms; Japanese sets them)

  • context.analyzer (features/analyzedWords.ts, common/analyzer.ts) joins morphemes into words with rules from language.toml. It returns the word as match and its parts as segments, and it finds:
    • phrases over several words, kana spellings included (かもしれない → かも知れない; によると before the piece による);
    • compounds the splitter keeps whole (年前, 明治天皇);
    • numbers with their counter (27日);
    • the word holding most of a piece that starts inside another (思っ|たより → より);
    • the analyzer's kanji lemma for a kana word whose own entries lack its part of speech (もの → 物).
    Tests: analyzedWords.ja.test.ts (18). Each one fails without its rule.
  • FormSource.Redirect (passes/redirects.ts): Wiktionary's kana soft redirects become forms of the kanji lemmas read that way (つまり → 詰まり), for words with no entry of their own. A katakana spelling of a hiragana word always counts (ワクワク), and so does a kanji variant (国々). That's 34,035 forms, ranked with lemmas. Test: imported/forms.ja.test.ts.
  • Readings from ruby (rubyReading): 75,556 entries show kana under the headword. Tests in transform.test.ts.
  • Syllable entries rank last, like letters (は opens with the particle).

Language files

  • languages/ja/language.toml: the analyzer, its join rules and the upos map. grammar.tsv: 70 lines (endings by UniDic base form, particles, set phrases, fillers). profile.md: the profile and run log.

Outside this repo (uncommitted)

  • ../Protocol.md: segments may split the first entry's one-string match; Japanese example.
  • ../extension: isValidSplit accepts that split, and the popover marks the clicked part.
  • ../corpus/eval:
    • reference words from Sudachi (units.py, --units on collect and collect-browser);
    • a seeded click per word;
    • match kept, and the judge sees the word, the clicked piece and the underline (judge-prompt-units.md);
    • score lines for clicks on pieces;
    • repair.ts splits parts at Japanese punctuation, or without any.

What got worse, and why it stays

  • Kana words now reach obscure kanji words written alike through redirects, ranked after the right one but still listed: けれど also shows ケレド "Credo", はし five nouns. A redirect at the top is reordered by the analyzer's lemma (みる → 見る before 海松).
  • Compounds with no entry show their parts, not a meaning for the whole (江戸前 as 江戸 + 前).
  • Pieces the analyzer reads differently from the text's intent: brand names (プーマ → puma), slang (ブレイクる), transcript fragments.

False positives and hand patches

False positives (2), in ../corpus/eval/out/ja/false-positives.jsonl: a cut-off filler そ, and 短いよで, a transcript slip for 短いようで.

No hand-patch list. Of the 97 words the judge called missing (u11):

  • 32 turned out to be in the data and were fixed by the lookup and import changes above.
  • 65 are not in English Wiktionary. Most are compounds of words it has, units, abbreviations, fragments and rare terms. Only a few are common words a learner needs (分かれ道, 乗り心地, うーん).

Open

  • The content gate: 4 items short. The failures left are senses Wiktionary lacks (36), phrases (15) and endings shown only as their base (10).
  • Headword: する-verbs show as the noun (勉強 VERB); Wiktionary's canonical form is 勉強する.
