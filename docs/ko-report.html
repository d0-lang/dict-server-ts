Profile summary

  • Data. English Wiktionary dump of 2026-11-01, 64,457 Korean lines: 39,314 Hangul headwords, 22,386 hanja, 16,882 form-of lines. ko_en.sqlite has 40,499 entries. Particles and endings are there, written with hyphens (-에서, -었-), and verbs have conjugation tables. Many common Sino-Korean and loan words have no Hangul page.
  • Corpus. 28 texts, 71k words (eojeols). Spoken: 15 YouTube videos in 9 genres, auto-captions repaired by Haiku, 31.8k words. Written: 13 ko.wikipedia articles, 39.2k words. No news, blogs or guides.
  • Words. An eojeol is a word with its particles and endings (학교에서, 먹었습니다), and stems fuse with endings (했, 갈). The plain lookup found an entry for 49.9% of corpus words; the analyzer path finds one for about 95%.
  • Analyzer: Kiwi (kiwi-nlp 0.25.0), a Korean analyzer with a language model. Lindera's ko-dic, used in r1–r4, lacks 이 in its build and misread changed stems and bound nouns; working around it took four settings, now removed.
  • Grammar file. 112 lines: particles and endings Wiktionary lacks or only names (-이나 "or", quoting -이라고, -이란, -으면서 "while", polite -요 endings such as -잖아요), constructions over a space (-을 수 있다, -에 따라, 예를 들어), noun suffixes (-률, -별), bound nouns (데 "in doing"), spoken words (뭐, 막, 좀), and 10 hand patches for common nouns.
  • Headword. As Wiktionary writes it (먹다); no labels. The Revised Romanization is the headword's reading (import.readingTags: 먹다 meokda), for 39,443 of 40,499 entries.

Accepted rules and their unit tests

All in the context.analyzer settings of language.toml; the TypeScript names no language. Tests: apps/server/src/lookup/features/analyzedWords/lookup.ko.test.ts, 20 of them, on Kiwi, each checked to fail without its change.

Fused morphemes and base forms (baseSpellings, baseLetters)

Kiwi gives a fused syllable's morphemes at one place (했: 하 + 었); they are one piece with each one's base form, and VV+EP matches either tag. A stem is spelled with 다, a particle or ending with hyphens, stems first (걸 is 거, then -ㄹ). A morpheme with no letters joins the next (the copula in 냄새야).

finds a verb by its stem with 다, and its endings with hyphens (먹었습니다)
finds a particle by its hyphenated spelling, not a word spelled alike (에서 "Esau")
reads the copula Kiwi gives no letters of with the ending after it (냄새야)

Noun + 하다 (stemSuffixes), changed stems, 이

reads a noun with 하다 as the verb (공부했어요 → 공부하다)
finds a verb whose stem changes (들었는데 → 듣다)
reads 이 after a noun as a particle (생각이; 호랑이 stays whole)
reads 이 at a word's start before a particle as the pronoun (이를)

Compounds (compoundPos)

Nouns written together are one word, looked up whole (양자역학, 김장), else by the longest parts Wiktionary has (발색|단). A noun Kiwi doesn't know is cut into Wiktionary's nouns when the last piece is one and no piece is a single letter (간송|미술관; not the name 김|연수).

reads nouns written together as the compound
shows a noun Kiwi doesn't know by the nouns in it

Endings in a longer click (spacedWords), fused endings

A click over several words (처음이니까: 처음 + the copula word 이니까) shows each word with an ending by its morphemes, even when Wiktionary lists the word as a table form, since the ending carries the meaning: -니까 "because", -면 "if", -죠 "right?". Not a word made with a suffix or counter (학계, 18개월). An ending fused of several is each of them, grammar lines first: 였 is -이다 + -었-, 신 -으시- + -ㄴ, 라는 -이라는 "called" + -이다. Rows of the word as written that don't fit Kiwi's reading go when some do (이다 "to carry on the head" for the copula).

shows each ending of a word in a longer click
shows each ending fused into one syllable (오신)
shows what an ending adds to the copula fused into it (수도라는)

Auxiliary verbs (auxiliaries)

An auxiliary written onto the verb makes a verb Wiktionary often has, ranked before the head's own: 전해|진다고 is 전해지다 "to be passed down", 도와|줬어요 도와주다.

reads an auxiliary verb with the verb before it

Constructions over a space

The phrase matcher takes words a space apart, and a phrase may start at an ending or particle written with its hyphen: 갈 수 있다 (가 + ㄹ) is -ㄹ 수 있다 "can", 급수에 따라 -에 따라 "according to", 오기 때문에 -기 때문에 "because". Such a popover has no segments: the protocol's segments split the match, which here runs over several words. Wiktionary has few of these, so they are grammar lines.

finds a construction over words a space apart, from an ending

Spaces (spacedWords), ranking (ranksByPos), counters

takes no single verb for a phrase, with spaces (우리는, not 우리다)
puts a headword spelled as written first, not a noun spelled alike (하지만, 다른)
ranks entries by Kiwi's part of speech (우리 "we")
reads the word after a number as a counter (3번)
reads a particle after Latin letters as the particle (TV가)
reads no word across a space (Kiwi's multi-word dictionary is off)

Romanized reading (import.readingTags), pointer glosses (import.pointerWords)

Other languages

Every Japanese corpus lookup on the v0.1.3-rc.1 dictionaries, main against this branch (r12): 31 of 72,686 responses change, no first entry. Words headed by the copula lose junk verbs (煮る, 似る, 出る). No other language has an analyzer.

What got worse, and why it stays

  • Kiwi's own misreadings: 아니하고 as 아니 + 하고, 식이 with 이 the determiner, 분기 "quarter" as 분 + 기, some names cut (박종훈).
  • Unknown words that showed some part by chance under ko-dic and now show none (카페올, 네빌).
  • More entries per popover: a word's endings now each show their line, so a popover is longer (감소이다: 감소, the copula, -다). The judge rated a few of these as clutter.
  • Tried and dropped: Wiktionary's nouns as Kiwi's own words. At the default score they turned 이제, 다시, 모두 into nouns (r6: 71 better, 64 worse); only the 5,162 Kiwi lacks, at a low score, worked, and finding them takes a second Kiwi build at startup (1.3 GB peak).

No false positives were added. Hand-patched in grammar.tsv: 이산화, 인슐린, 이온, 회로, 농도, 효율, 생성, 억제, 불순물, 줄기세포. Missing elsewhere in the sample, and not patched: 엔지니어, 픽셀, 결제하다, 역대, 일부분, 실온, 냉동실.

Open questions

  • Grammar and random gates. Both fail on data: English Wiktionary lacks about one word in five that a reader meets, and rare senses of common grammar words. Passing them needs a hand-written word list on that scale, or another source.
  • Kiwi's memory. Its WebAssembly build loads the model unquantized; a quantized load would cut it.
  • For a native speaker: the grammar file's lines and examples; whether determiners spelled like verb forms should lead (오른).
  • Tier 2: more constructions over a space (only the common ones have lines); numbers with counters in one click (2024년에).
