Profile summary

  • Data. English Wiktionary dump of 2026-11-01, 64,457 Korean lines: 39,314 Hangul headwords, 22,386 hanja, 16,882 form-of lines. ko_en.sqlite has 40,499 entries. Particles and endings are there, written with hyphens (-에서, -었-), and verbs have conjugation tables. Many common Sino-Korean and loan words have no Hangul page (농도, 회로, 엔지니어, 픽셀).
  • Corpus. 28 texts, 71k words (eojeols). Spoken: 15 YouTube videos in 9 genres, auto-captions repaired by Haiku, 31.8k words. Written: 13 ko.wikipedia articles in 10 topics, 39.2k words. No news, blogs or guides.
  • Words. An eojeol is a word with its particles and endings (학교에서, 먹었습니다), and stems fuse with endings (했, 갈). The plain lookup found an entry for 49.9% of corpus words; the analyzer path (Lindera's ko-dic) finds one for 95.3%.
  • Lindera's ko-dic has no 이: the subject particle, the copula and the pronoun are missing from its build (mecab-ko-dic has 30 entries for it), so 생각이 was 생|각이. The restore setting puts it back.
  • Grammar file. 57 lines: particles and endings Wiktionary lacks or only names (-이나 "or", quoting -이라고, polite -요 endings, -적), bound nouns (데 "in doing"), spoken words (뭐, 막, 좀), and 10 hand patches for common nouns.
  • Headword. As Wiktionary writes it (먹다); no labels. The Revised Romanization is the headword's reading (import.readingTags: 먹다 meokda), for 39,443 of 40,499 entries.

Accepted rules and their unit tests

All in the context.analyzer settings of language.toml; the TypeScript names no language. Tests: apps/server/src/lookup/features/analyzedWords/lookup.ko.test.ts, each checked to fail without its setting.

Parts of speech and base forms (posFields, fused, baseSpellings, baseLetters)

ko-dic's tag is its first field; VV+EP is a fused morpheme with both tags. A stem is spelled with 다, a particle or ending with hyphens; a fused morpheme takes its parts' base forms, stems first (걸 is 거, then -ㄹ).

finds a verb by its stem with 다, and its endings with hyphens (먹었습니다)
finds a particle by its hyphenated spelling, not a word spelled alike (에서 "Esau")

Noun + 하다 (stemSuffixes), changed stems (stemForms)

reads a noun with 하다 as the verb (공부했어요 → 공부하다)
finds a verb whose stem changes by its table forms (들었는데 → 듣다)

The missing 이 (restore)

reads 이 after a noun as a particle, though ko-dic lacks it (생각이; 호랑이 stays whole)
reads 이 at a word's start before a particle as the pronoun (이를, not 이르다)

Spaces (spacedWords), ranking (ranksByPos), adnominal endings (recheck)

takes no single verb for a phrase, with spaces (우리는, not 우리다)
puts a headword spelled as written first, not a noun spelled alike (하지만, 다른; 하지 is 하다)
ranks entries by ko-dic's part of speech (우리 "we"; 년 "year")
reads a particle after Latin letters as the particle (TV가)

Romanized reading (import.readingTags)

takes a reading from a form tagged as one (먹다: meokda)

Pointer glosses (import.pointerWords)

"Yang-vowel form of -어서", "Post-vowel and post-liquid allomorphic form of -으면", "topic-marked form of 나" become links to the word, so the reader gets its meaning. About 120 senses.

links a gloss that only names a form in the language's terms (-아서, -면; not 제, whose gloss says when; not in Polish)

Other languages

Every Japanese corpus lookup on the v0.1.3-rc.1 dictionaries, main against this branch: 31 of 72,686 responses change, no first entry. Words headed by the copula lose junk verbs (煮る, 似る, 出る). No other language has an analyzer.

What got worse, and why it stays

Classified diff r1 → r3 on the gate sample: 255 better, 98 worse, 345 same. Every "worse" was read, and the systematic ones were fixed in r4 (the determiner reading of a particle 이, the ending -어 as the interjection, 년, 감사합니다, 좋아하는, 이고, 그것이).

  • ko-dic misreadings with no general fix: 전 "whole" as 저 + -ㄴ, 나서 (-고 나서) as 나 "I", 박은 as 박 "gourd", 가지 "kind" as 가지다, 번 "time" as 벌다.
  • A determiner spelled like a verb form leads where the verb is meant (오른 "right" for "rose"): the price of 다른 "other" and 그런 "such" leading.
  • Both readings of a changed stem show, in ko-dic's order (들었는데: 들다, then 듣다).

Open questions

  • Hand patches or not. English Wiktionary lacks about one word in five that a reader meets. Passing tier 1 needs a hand-written word list on that scale, or another source.
  • The analyzer. ko-dic misreads common words, and Lindera's build lacks 이. Kiwi read both right in the eval units. Kiwi is a separate analyzer, not a Lindera dictionary, with its own WebAssembly build (kiwi-nlp). Report the 이 bug to Lindera? Measure Kiwi as a second backend?
  • For a native speaker: the grammar file's lines and examples; whether 오른-style determiners should lead.
  • Tier 2: constructions over eojeols (-ㄹ 수 있다, -고 있다, 예를 들어) need the phrase matcher to allow spaces; numbers with counters and units.
